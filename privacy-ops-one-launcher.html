Plain-language summary: OpsOne Launcher is a home screen that works on your phone. It has no account, no ads, and no analytics. What it learns about your habits stays on your device, and you can clear it at any time. The only thing the app can send off your device is an optional crash report, which is off unless you turn it on.

1. About This Policy

This policy explains how the OpsOne Launcher Android app (package com.vtechs.opsone.launcher) handles your information. The app is developed by Vortex Technologies Limited (“we”, “us”). It adds to the Vortex Technologies Privacy Policy; where the two differ, this policy applies to OpsOne Launcher.

OpsOne Launcher replaces your phone’s home screen. Most of what it does happens on your device, so this policy is mostly about what the app reads on your phone and what it keeps there, not about data sent to us.

2. What We Collect

Nothing by default. The app has no account or sign-in, and we do not receive your contacts, notifications, app usage, launch history, or search terms. The app contains no advertising, analytics, or tracking code.

Optional crash reports. The app has Android’s internet permission for one purpose: sending crash reports, if you choose to turn them on (see Section 7). Until you do, the app does not connect to the internet.

3. What the App Reads on Your Device

To work as a home screen, the app reads the following on your phone. Items marked optional are only read if you grant that access, and the app works without them.

  • Installed apps and their shortcuts — to list, search, and launch your apps and to work out which app can handle an action such as call, message, or navigate. (Android permission: QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES.)
  • Contacts (optional) — your contacts’ names, phone numbers, email addresses, and photo thumbnails. They are used to show your starred contacts, to let you call or message them from the People area, and to match a chat in a messaging app to the right contact (by name, phone number, or email address, on your device). Contact details are read when needed and are not stored by the app, except an internal contact reference in your on-device launch history. (READ_CONTACTS, asked for only after an explanation.)
  • Chat shortcuts — messaging apps offer Android a short list of your recent chats (a name and a link) so other apps can share to them. The app reads that list to show recent people on the People screen. It does not read messages.
  • Notification access (optional) — Android only shares “now playing” media controls with apps that have notification access, so the launcher uses it to show and control what’s playing, including album art. It also lets the app show a small marker, with a count, on a favourite contact who has sent you a message that you haven’t read yet. For that it reads the sender’s name from the notification title, in memory, and nothing else. The message text is never read. Notification content is not displayed, stored, or sent anywhere. The app keeps only which app posted a notification and when.
  • Usage access (optional) — which apps you opened in the last 14 days, used on your device to suggest apps from day one. It is read when suggestions are calculated and is never stored.
  • Wallpaper — the app can set your lock-screen wallpaper or your home wallpaper to match the theme you choose, only when you tap the button for it. (SET_WALLPAPER)

The app also uses these standard permissions: SET_WALLPAPER (only when you tap “Set as lock screen” or “Set matching home wallpaper”), SET_ALARM (to start timers you ask for), and VIBRATE (touch feedback). Internet access (INTERNET) is used only for optional crash reports. It does not request location, calendar, call log, SMS, phone state, microphone, camera, or storage permissions. Calls and messages are handed to your dialer or messaging app, where you complete them.

You can withdraw contacts, notification access, or usage access at any time in Android Settings.

4. What the App Keeps on Your Device

The app stores the following in its private storage on your phone:

  • Launch history — what you open through the launcher (the action, time of day, weekday or weekend, and whether it worked). This is used to rank people and apps and to make suggestions. An action that opens a contact includes an internal contact reference.
  • Notification signals — which app posted a notification and when (never the content).
  • Your feedback on suggestions (“Not now”, “Don’t suggest”), where you’ve placed apps, your theme, and other settings.
  • Your settings — your theme and accent, Dark or Light, clock format, where you have placed apps, and whether you have completed the welcome screens.
  • Cached images — app icons and small album-art images, used to draw the screen.

Suggestions are calculated on your device. Each suggestion can show why it was made (“Why this?”).

5. Retention and Deletion

  • Launch history, notification signals, and suggestion feedback are deleted automatically after 180 days.
  • You can view recent launches and delete all learning data at any time in Launcher settings → Learning data → Clear learning data. Your app placements and theme are kept.
  • Clearing the app’s storage in Android Settings, or uninstalling the app, deletes everything it has stored on the device.

Backups: the app is excluded from Android’s backup and phone-transfer features, so what it has learned is not copied to your Google account or to a new phone. A new phone starts fresh.

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to delete on our side. You don’t need to use our Data Deletion Request page for this app.

6. Sharing

None, apart from the optional crash reports in Section 7. The app does not sell, share, or upload your data, and contains no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs. Fonts are built into the app, and it does not check for updates over the network (updates come through Google Play).

7. Optional Crash Reports

OpsOne Launcher can send crash reports to help us fix bugs. They are off unless you turn them on: the app asks once, when you first use it, and you can change your mind at any time in Launcher settings → About → Crash reports. If you turn them off, reports that are waiting to be sent are deleted.

A report contains technical details only: the error and where it happened in the app’s code, the app version, the Android version, your device model, the theme in use, and whether the app had trouble starting. The app removes anything that could identify a person or an action from the error text before sending (for example contact references, phone numbers, email addresses, and file locations).

A report never includes your contacts, notifications, launch history, list of installed apps, app usage, search text, screenshots, or account details, and the app does not send your IP address. Reports are sent over an encrypted connection to our crash-reporting provider, Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., United States), where we keep them for as long as needed to fix the problem.

You can ask us to delete reports (see Section 9).

8. Children

OpsOne Launcher is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

9. Your Rights

You have the rights described in the Your Rights section of the Vortex Technologies Privacy Policy, including access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, and objection. Because the app keeps your data only on your device, you control it directly through the in-app and Android settings described above. Crash reports contain no information that identifies you, but if you believe one relates to you, email us and we will look for it and delete it where we can.

To exercise any of these rights for OpsOne Launcher, email info@vtechs.com.jm or use our Data Deletion Request page. We respond within 30 days and may ask you to verify your identity first. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Information Commissioner in Jamaica or the data protection authority where you live.

10. Changes and Contact

We update this policy when OpsOne Launcher changes the way it handles data — for example, when a feature, permission, or service provider is added or removed. The “Last Updated” date at the top shows when it last changed. For material changes we will also give notice in the app or by email where we can.

Questions about this policy: info@vtechs.com.jm.
