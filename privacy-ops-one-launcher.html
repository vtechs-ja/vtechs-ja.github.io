Plain-language summary: Ops One Launcher is a home screen that works entirely on your phone. It has no account, no ads, and no analytics, and the current version cannot connect to the internet at all. What it learns about your habits stays on your device, and you can clear it at any time.

1. About This Policy

This policy explains how the Ops One Launcher Android app (package com.vtechs.opsone.launcher) handles your information. The app is developed by Vortex Technologies Limited (“we”, “us”). It adds to the Vortex Technologies Privacy Policy; where the two differ, this policy applies to Ops One Launcher.

Ops One Launcher replaces your phone’s home screen. Most of what it does happens on your device, so this policy is mostly about what the app reads on your phone and what it keeps there, not about data sent to us.

2. What We Collect

Nothing. The app has no account or sign-in. Release builds do not have Android’s internet permission, so the operating system prevents the app from sending anything off your device. We do not receive your contacts, notifications, app usage, launch history, or search terms, and the app contains no advertising, analytics, or tracking code.

Planned: optional crash reports. A future version will offer crash reporting that is off unless you turn it on. See Section 7. We will update this policy before that version is released.

3. What the App Reads on Your Device

To work as a home screen, the app reads the following on your phone. Items marked optional are only read if you grant that access, and the app works without them.

  • Installed apps and their shortcuts — to list, search, and launch your apps and to work out which app can handle an action such as call, message, or navigate. (Android permission: QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES.)
  • Contacts (optional) — your starred contacts’ names, numbers, and photo thumbnails, so you can call or message them from the People area. Contact details are read when needed and are not stored by the app, except an internal contact reference in your on-device launch history. (READ_CONTACTS, asked for only after an explanation.)
  • Notification access (optional) — Android only shares “now playing” media controls with apps that have notification access, so the launcher uses it to show and control what’s playing, including album art. Notification content is not displayed, stored, or sent anywhere. The app keeps only which app posted a notification and when.
  • Usage access (optional) — which apps you opened in the last 14 days, used on your device to suggest apps from day one. It is read when suggestions are calculated and is never stored.

The app also uses these standard permissions: SET_WALLPAPER (only when you tap “Set as lock screen”), SET_ALARM (to start timers you ask for), and VIBRATE (touch feedback). It does not request location, calendar, call log, SMS, phone state, microphone, camera, or storage permissions. Calls and messages are handed to your dialer or messaging app, where you complete them.

You can withdraw contacts, notification access, or usage access at any time in Android Settings.

4. What the App Keeps on Your Device

The app stores the following in its private storage on your phone:

  • Launch history — what you open through the launcher (the action, time of day, weekday or weekend, and whether it worked). This is used to rank people and apps and to make suggestions. An action that opens a contact includes an internal contact reference.
  • Notification signals — which app posted a notification and when (never the content).
  • Your feedback on suggestions (“Not now”, “Don’t suggest”), where you’ve placed apps, your theme, and other settings.
  • Cached images — app icons and small album-art images, used to draw the screen.

Suggestions are calculated on your device. Each suggestion can show why it was made (“Why this?”).

5. Retention and Deletion

  • Launch history, notification signals, and suggestion feedback are deleted automatically after 180 days.
  • You can view recent launches and delete all learning data at any time in Launcher settings → Learning data → Clear learning data. Your app placements and theme are kept.
  • Clearing the app’s storage in Android Settings, or uninstalling the app, deletes everything it has stored on the device.

Android device backups: if you use Android’s backup or phone-transfer features, Android may include the app’s on-device data in your backup, which is stored by Google under your Google account, and restore it to a new phone. We cannot access these backups.

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to delete on our side. You don’t need to use our Data Deletion Request page for this app.

6. Sharing

None. The app does not sell, share, or upload your data, and contains no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs. Fonts are built into the app, and it does not check for updates over the network (updates come through Google Play).

7. Optional Crash Reports (Coming Later)

A future version will let you choose to send crash reports to help us fix bugs. When it arrives:

  • Crash reports will be off unless you turn them on, and you can turn them off at any time; reports that are waiting to be sent are then discarded.
  • A report will contain technical details only: the error and stack trace, app version, Android version, device model, and the theme in use. It will be sent to our crash-reporting provider, Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., United States).
  • Reports will never include your contacts, notifications, launch history, app list, app usage, search text, screenshots, or IP address.

We will update this policy and the Google Play data-safety section before that version is released.

8. Children

Ops One Launcher is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

9. Your Rights

You have the rights described in the Your Rights section of the Vortex Technologies Privacy Policy, including access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, and objection. Because the app keeps your data only on your device, you control it directly through the in-app and Android settings described above.

To exercise any of these rights for Ops One Launcher, email info@vtechs.com.jm or use our Data Deletion Request page. We respond within 30 days and may ask you to verify your identity first. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Information Commissioner in Jamaica or the data protection authority where you live.

10. Changes and Contact

We update this policy when Ops One Launcher changes the way it handles data — for example, when a feature, permission, or service provider is added or removed. The “Last Updated” date at the top shows when it last changed. For material changes we will also give notice in the app or by email where we can.

Questions about this policy: info@vtechs.com.jm.
